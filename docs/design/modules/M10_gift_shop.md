# M10: Gift Shop

**Target vocab (30):** yo-yo, doll, kite, ball, card, gift, flower, one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, dollar, how much, how many, want, for, thank, box, bag, grandmother, sister, teacher
**Off-list words needed:** buy, pay, price, coin, more, put
**Difficulty:** A1–B1 · **Time:** 60–120 s · **Route:** vocab-first (Toys + Holidays + Numbers)
**Status:** Draft (rev 2: rebuilt as a two-way number puzzle, with no serial lookup)

## The hook

The bomb wants to buy a **gift**, and it has to spend **exactly** the money in its wallet:
not one dollar more, not one dollar less. The **Defuser sees the price tags** but not the
wallet. The **Expert sees the wallet and the gift guide** but not the prices. Neither of them
can work out the answer alone, so the numbers have to go back and forth.

## Inspiration

The "divide and defuse" idea, where *each* player holds half the facts (the information gap
runs both ways, not one), crossed with shopkeeper play. Most modules in this set have a
Defuser who describes and an Expert who instructs. Here both sides ask and both sides answer:
*"How much is the kite?" "Five dollars." "How many dollars do we have?" "Nine."*

## Defuser view

- **Wish bubble:** a picture of who the gift is for: grandmother, sister, teacher (plus
  father, brother and a friend at B1, reusing M01 art).
- **Shelf with price tags:** yo-yo, doll, kite, ball, flower. Each tag shows the price **as
  an English word**: `seven dollars`, not `$7` (see English lock). Prices change every round. At B1 two items share a type at different prices (a big
  kite and a small kite).
- **Card rack:** ❤ heart card, ⭐ star card, **THANK YOU** card, each with a price.
- **Wrap station:** gift **box** or gift **bag**, each with a price.
- **Basket** with a count badge per item, plus **PAY**.

## Expert manual

The Expert's screen shows two things.

**1. The wallet:** `The bomb has nine dollars.` (any amount from three to twelve, always
written as a word)

**2. The gift guide:**

> **For grandmother:** flowers (one, two or three) and a heart card. Put it in a box or a bag.
> **For my sister:** she wants a doll or a kite. Put it in a bag.
> **For the teacher:** the THANK YOU card, and one more thing. No box, no bag.
> **For my father** *(B1)*: balls, as many as you want, and a star card.
> **For my friend** *(B1)*: a yo-yo, and a box or a bag.

> **Rules:**
> Spend **all** the money. Not more, not less.
> There is only **one** good gift. Find it!

## Solving it

1. D: *"A picture of an old woman."* E: *"For grandmother: flowers, a heart card, a box or a
   bag. How much is the flower?"*
2. D: *"Two dollars. The heart card is one dollar. The box is three. The bag is two."*
3. E (wallet = 9): flowers can be 2, 4 or 6, plus a 1-dollar card, plus 3 or 2 for the wrap.
   *"One flower: two, one… three, then the box is six, the bag is five. No. Two flowers: four,
   one, five… box eight, bag seven. No! Three flowers: six, one, seven… the bag is two… nine!"*
   Answer: three flowers + heart card + bag.
4. D builds the basket and presses PAY. A wrong total is a strike, and the shelf reshuffles
   its prices (so the team can't just try every combination).

What's different from the other modules:

- **No table to look up.** The Expert's work is **mental arithmetic aloud** with the
  Defuser's numbers. All twelve number words come up naturally, because they're both prices
  and running totals.
- **The questions go both ways.** *How much* (price) and *how many* (quantity) are asked by
  *both* players.
- **The generator guarantees exactly one combination** that hits the wallet, so the team has
  to search together, not guess.

## Example round

> **D:** The picture is a teacher.
> **E:** For the teacher: the thank you card and one more thing. How much is the thank you card?
> **D:** Three dollars.
> **E:** We have eight dollars. Eight… three… five. What is five dollars?
> **D:** The kite is five! The doll is six. The ball is four.
> **E:** The kite! Thank you card and kite.
> **D:** Pay!

## English lock

With digits, the numbers could go back and forth entirely in Chinese. So both sides show
prices and the wallet **only as English words** (`seven dollars`, `The bomb has nine
dollars`). Those numbers are the information being passed, so reading the English is the
only way to get them. The basket's count badges stay as digits: they're the Defuser's own
check, not something passed to the Expert.

## Information gap

✅ **Two-way split.** Prices are only on the Defuser's side, and the wallet and gift guide are
only on the Expert's side. No serial number.

## Difficulty scaling

- **A1:** grandmother/teacher only, 2 parts per gift, wallet ≤ 6, single-digit prices. The
  answer always uses one of each.
- **A2:** all A-level recipients, quantity choices (one to three flowers), wallet ≤ 12.
- **B1:** father and friend, unlimited quantities, same-type items at different prices (big
  kite, small kite). Two-step combinations are needed.

## Build notes

- Generate the prices, then **enumerate every combination** the guide allows. Keep the board
  only if exactly one combination equals the wallet.
- The THANK YOU card is the only written English on the Defuser's side. That's on purpose:
  the Defuser has to read "thank" to find it.
- Recipients and guide lines are teacher-editable data.

## Open questions

- **400-list tier:** the 400-word list adds *thirteen–ninety*, *hundred* and *first/second/third*. A higher tier could use bigger prices written out in full (`seven hundred dollars`, `forty-five dollars`) on the Expert's side. That makes the running total much harder to do in your head, so the numbers really have to be read.

- Should the Expert be allowed a scratch-pad on screen for running totals? It helps lower
  levels, but at B1 it takes away the "say the numbers aloud" pressure.
