# M08: Mystery Guest

**Target vocab (22):** who, what, yes, no, not, can, do/does/did, have/has, like, play, dance, sing, read, write, draw, he is, she is, name, kite, ball, yo-yo, doll
**Off-list words needed:** ask, question, guest, party, send, true, notes
**Difficulty:** A2–B2 · **Time:** 90–150 s · **Route:** vocab-first (grammar: auxiliaries, yes/no, Ability & Talent)
**Status:** Draft (rev 2: everything is enforced by the game; nothing depends on players choosing to follow a rule)

## The hook

The bomb maker is hiding at a party of eight guests. **Only the bomb knows who it is.** The
Defuser builds yes/no questions from word tiles and sends them to the bomb. **The bomb's
answers appear only on the Expert's screen**, so the Expert has to read each answer aloud. The
bomb only allows a few questions, and it rejects badly built ones. Find the guest before the
questions run out.

## Inspiration

Deduction by questioning, a party-game staple, rebuilt so that the **game enforces every
rule**: no honor system. The tile-built questions come from the "divide and defuse" idea (each
player holds part of the process). The grammar target is **forming questions with can / does
/ did / is**, and the bomb checks it, not the players.

## Defuser view

- A party scene: **8 guests**, each with a **name tag** (AMY, BEN, TOM, MAY, LEO, EVA, KAI,
  SUE). Each guest's look shows:
  - **Can:** dancing shoes / a microphone / a book / a pen and paper / a paintbrush (dance /
    sing / read / write / draw)
  - **Has:** holding a kite, a ball, a yo-yo or a doll (or nothing)
  - **Likes:** a heart bubble over one thing, such as a heart over a ball (= likes to play
    ball)
  - **Did:** a finished drawing pinned up behind them, or a written letter in their hand (=
    did draw / did write today)
- Tap a guest to gray them out, and tap again to undo.
- **Question builder:** four tile rows. Tap one tile per row, then **SEND**.
  - Row 1: `Can` `Does` `Did` `Is` `What` `Who`
  - Row 2: `he` `she`
  - Row 3: `dance` `sing` `read` `write` `draw` `play` `have` `like`
  - Row 4: `a kite` `a ball` `a yo-yo` `a doll` `today` *(blank allowed)*
- **Question counter** (for example 5 ❓). It goes down automatically on each SEND.
- **ACCUSE** button: then tap the guest.

The Defuser **never sees the bomb's answers**. They only appear on the Expert's screen.

## Expert screen

- **Witness notes:** two free facts about the bomb maker, shown at the start:
  > *His name is BEN, KAI or LEO.*
  > *He can not sing.*
- **Answer feed:** every question the Defuser sends appears here with the bomb's answer as a
  full sentence:
  > **Q:** Can he dance? → **No, he can not.**
  > **Q:** Does he have a kite? → **Yes, he does.**
  > **Q:** Did he draw today? → **No, he did not.**
  > **Q:** Does he can dance? → **That is not a good question.** *(the question is still used up)*
  > **Q:** What he like? → **Ask a yes or no question.** *(used up)*
- The Expert **never sees the secret guest**. They can't give the answer away, because they
  don't know it either.

## Solving it

- The Expert reads the witness notes. The name line cuts 8 guests to 3, but only if the
  Defuser finds the right name tags.
- The Defuser builds a question and sends it. The answer only appears on the Expert's screen,
  so the Expert has to read it aloud for the team to make progress.
- **Bad grammar costs a question.** "Does he can dance?" or "Can he have a kite?" is
  rejected, and the question is gone. Tile order is fixed (auxiliary → pronoun → verb →
  object), so the only mistake possible is *choosing the wrong words*, which is exactly the
  grammar point. "Does" goes with *have/like/play*, "Can" goes with abilities, and "Did" goes
  with *draw/write today*.
- **What/Who tiles always fail.** Students learn the difference between yes/no questions and
  wh-questions by losing a question once.
- **Accusing wrong** is a strike plus 1 ❓.

Nothing here depends on honesty. The counter, the grammar check, the hidden answers and the
hidden target are all enforced by the game. The Expert *could* read only "yes" instead of the
full sentence, and that's fine. The full sentence is always on their screen as a model.

## Example round

> **E:** The notes say: His name is Ben, Kai or Leo. He can not sing.
> **D:** Ben, Kai, Leo… Kai has a microphone. Not Kai. *(grays out)* OK, I send: "Does he have a kite?"
> **E:** It says… "Yes, he does."
> **D:** Ben has a kite… Leo has a kite, too. I send: "Did he draw today?"
> **E:** "No, he did not."
> **D:** Leo has a picture. It is Ben! *(accuse)*

## Information gap

✅ **The answer exists only inside the bomb.** The Defuser has the guests and the questions,
and the Expert has the answers and the notes. Each needs the other, and neither can leak
something they don't have.

## Difficulty scaling

- **A2:** 6 guests, only `Can` + abilities active (other tiles grayed), 6 ❓, no notes.
- **B1:** 8 guests, `Can` / `Does` with have/like/play, name note on, 5 ❓.
- **B2:** `Did … today` questions, 4 ❓, and the serial rule: *if the serial has a 7, one
  witness note is not true. It is the note with "can not".* The team has to notice that
  the answers contradict a note.

## Build notes

- The bomb's answer engine needs a small grammar table: which auxiliary goes with which
  verb, and which objects fit which verbs. Every valid combination gets a truthful
  full-sentence answer, and everything else gets a rejection line.
- Generate 8 guests, choose a target, then check the puzzle is solvable within the question
  budget (an information check: the best question sequence must isolate the target within N
  questions).
- Name tags need large, high-contrast letters.

## Open questions

- Should a rejected question cost a full ❓ at A2, or should A2 get one free mistake so new
  learners aren't punished while learning the tiles?
